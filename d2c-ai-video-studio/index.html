The short answer

Yes, it can work, if you sell finished ads and ideas instead of editing hours

  • Demand is real and growing fast

    Upwork freelancer earnings for AI video generation and editing grew 329% in 2025, the fastest of any AI skill. Fiverr searches for "AI UGC video ads" grew 265% in six months. Meta's ad system now rewards many different ads, so brands spending $10k–$50k a month on Meta launch about 18 new ads a month, and the best of them about 35.

  • Plain "AI video editing" is getting cheap

    On Upwork, new contracts for generative AI and creative work grew 90% in a year, but earnings per contract fell 13%. Offshore editors deliver 10–50 videos a week for $950–$3,000 a month. Few companies hire full-time AI video editors: about 20 remote job posts on Glassdoor in June 2026. So sell finished ads with the script and the strategy included, and never sell hours.

  • There is a gap between $500 and $4,000 a month

    Cheap AI services sell 4–30 template videos for $395–$499. Agencies start at €4,000–$7,500 a month, often with 3–12 month contracts. Almost nobody in between sells strategy, scripts and AI production together, and none of the studios I found sells cinematic AI ads as a monthly plan. That middle is your place, with Velrun's film quality as the difference.

  • Sell to US, UK and Western European brands

    They pay about twice as much for the same work. Use local Eastern European brands for your first case studies if they are easier to reach.

  • Your time is the real cost, not the AI

    AI credits are about $30 per ad. Your hours are 80–95% of the cost. Working alone you can serve about 3 clients: roughly $7,000 a month in sales and $6,000 profit with international clients, or about €2,200 profit with local ones. To grow past that you need freelancers.

  • Follow the AI rules from day one

    Fake AI "customers" are illegal in the US, UK and EU. Under the EU AI Act, realistic AI people in ads must be labeled from 2 August 2026. Doing this right is also a selling point, because brands are afraid of getting it wrong.

Yes. The number of jobs is growing fast, and the price per job is falling

Two things are true at the same time. Brands need far more video ads than before, and more of those ads are made with AI. The money is in the part AI can't do alone: choosing what to say, writing it, and learning from the results.

Why brands need so many ads now

In December 2024 Meta launched Andromeda, a new system that chooses which ad each person sees. It rewards many different ideas. Near-copies of one ad get grouped and count as one. How do you make scripts worth paying for?

Sell the thinking, not the words

AI writes a script in seconds, and Fiverr sellers charge $35–$40. Professionals still get $150–$800 per script, and one supplement brand pays $400 per test script plus $500–$1,000 for each script that wins. They pay for research, angles and testing. That's what you sell.

What's in one script

  • Header: goal, customer type, awareness stage, angle, offer, product claims and their proof.
  • 3 hooks from 3 different angles, not 3 rewordings.
  • Body and call to action.
  • Voiceover, word for word, and on-screen text.
  • Shot list written for AI video: shots of 3–8 seconds, product shots from real photos.
  • 15 s and 30 s versions.
  • Notes for the editor or the creator.

What wins on Meta in 2026

  • Formats with the most winners (Motion, 550k+ ads): offer-first, demo, testimonial, unboxing, montage, listicle, split-screen, us-vs-them, before and after. Before and after gets rejected often in health and beauty.
  • Hooks used by accounts spending $1M+ a month: confession, bold claim, relatability, contrast, curiosity.
  • Good numbers: hook rate 30% or more (people who watch the first 3 seconds), hold rate about 25%. About 5% of ads become winners.
  • Scripted "testimonial" UGC now feels predictable. Street interviews, skits, expert comments and founder stories are working.

Your monthly process

  1. Research. Customer reviews, Reddit, Amazon and TikTok comments, competitors' ads in the Meta Ad Library.
  2. Angle map. Customer types × awareness stage (from "doesn't know the problem" to "ready to buy") × desire.
  3. Scripts. One test idea per concept, 3 hooks each.
  4. Production. Nothing is generated before the script is approved.
  5. Testing. The brand or its media buyer runs the new ads in a test campaign and judges them after 3–7 days.
  6. Next batch. About 60% new versions of winners, 30% winners in new formats, 10% brand-new ideas.
  7. Monthly memo. What won, what we learned, what we test next.

Why brands pay you instead of using ChatGPT

  • Better input: real customer words, ad results, support tickets, reasons for returns.
  • Scripts made for AI production: shot length, product consistency, lip sync.
  • Truly different ideas, which Meta's system rewards.
  • A legal check on every claim.
  • Accountability: you report the share of winning ads against the ~5% average. Optional: a bonus for each winning ad.

What's legal?

AI ads are legal. Fake customers are not

Most of the risk comes from AI people who look like real customers, and from health or beauty claims. This is research, not legal advice. Ask a lawyer to check your contract before your first clients sign.

Dates that matter

  1. 21 Oct 2024

    US: the FTC rule on reviews and testimonials takes effect. It bans testimonials from people who don't exist, including AI-made ones. Fines go up to $53,088 per violation.

  2. 6 Apr 2025

    UK: the DMCC Act bans fake reviews and paying for them.

  3. 22 Dec 2025

    US: the FTC sends its first warning letters under the rule, to 10 companies.

  4. Jun 2026

    New York: a law requiring ads to disclose AI "synthetic performers" takes effect (reported; check before use).

  5. 2 Aug 2026

    EU: AI Act Article 50 applies. Realistic AI-made people and scenes must be labeled. It was not delayed. Fines go up to €15M or 3% of turnover.

  6. 27 Sep 2026

    EU: vague green claims that can't be proven are banned (Directive 2024/825). This matters for scripts.

  7. 2 Dec 2026

    EU: AI tool makers must add machine-readable marks to their outputs.

✓Do

  • Write avatar ads as brand presenter ads, not as customer testimonials.
  • If an AI presenter repeats a real review, keep the review, its date and the customer's permission on file, and say "AI presenter" on screen and in the audio.
  • Label realistic AI people for EU, UK and New York audiences, and turn on each platform's AI disclosure setting.
  • Keep the tools' watermarks and C2PA metadata in the files.
  • Use licensed stock avatars and voices. Get written consent from any real person.
  • Get the client to sign off the proof for every product claim before you produce.
  • Use tools with clear commercial terms. Use Google Vertex AI for hero shots.

✕Don't

  • Invent customer stories, star ratings or numbers like "10,000 happy customers".
  • Make AI doctors, nurses or experts.
  • Make AI before/after results, or AI-improved skin and bodies.
  • Use celebrity or creator lookalikes, soundalike voices, or cloned voices without consent.
  • Promise copyright in raw AI footage, or promise sales and ROAS.
  • Prompt for other brands' logos or characters, or a living artist's style.
  • Write "Are you struggling with…?" lines. Meta rejects ads that imply a viewer's health or personal traits.

In the EU you are usually the one who must label

Law firms reading the European Commission's July 2026 guidelines say the duty to label falls on whoever decides how AI is used. When you choose the tools and make the AI people, that's you, not the brand. Deliver labeled master files and write in the contract who does what.

Put this in your contract

  1. Scope: concepts, ads, lengths, formats and languages in each plan.
  2. Revisions, and what counts as a new concept.
  3. Delivery times, counted from a complete brief.
  4. Payment in advance, minimum term, notice period, pause rules.
  5. Rights: the client gets the deliverables on full payment "to the extent rights exist". Raw AI footage may not be protected by copyright (US Copyright Office, January 2025). The scripts and edits you make are.
  6. Licensed avatars, voices and music: what each license covers and for how long.
  7. Client promises: rights to their materials, proof for their claims, real reviews with permission.
  8. AI labels: who labels what. The client must not remove labels and must turn on platform AI disclosure.
  9. No performance guarantee: no promises on ROAS, click rates or ad approval.
  10. Liability limit (the fees of the last 3–12 months) and indemnities both ways.

How do you get the first clients?

Warm leads first, then agency partners, then cold email

Channels, in the order to try them

  6. Marketplaces for your first reviews. Upwork takes 0–15%, Contra 0%, Fiverr 20%. Fiverr also has an AI Video Hub for AI video directors.

Brand: give the new studio its own name and domain. Put "by the team behind Velrun" in the footer and on the About page. That borrows Velrun's credibility without making Velrun look cheap. Keep the two portfolios separate, and move your biggest clients up to Velrun commercials.

Prices: show them. For deals under about $10,000 a month, public prices build trust, and 67% of business buyers prefer to buy without talking to a salesperson (Gartner, 2026). Let people pay for the Test Batch and Starter online. Keep Scale behind a call.

Page order

  1. Hero: a vertical reel of your best ads, one line ("New AI video ads every week, scripts included"), buttons for prices and a call.
  2. Proof: client logos and results. Add them as you get them.
  3. How it works: brief → scripts → ads → test → next batch.
  4. Work by category: home, pet, food and drink, beauty.
  5. Prices: all plans, what counts as an ad, and the terms.
  6. Comparison: you vs UGC creators, an in-house hire, DIY AI tools.
  7. "How many ads does your budget need?" A small calculator based on the Motion table.
  8. AI promise: licensed presenters, labeled AI, no fake testimonials.
  9. FAQ.
  10. Footer: "by the team behind Velrun".

What do you do first?

The first 90 days

  1. Days 1–14

    Build. Name, domain, logo. Contract with AI, likeness and rights clauses. Make 12 spec ads across 4 product categories for your portfolio. Set up Stripe, a client portal and Frame.io.

  2. Days 15–30

    Launch. Website live with prices. A list of 1,000–2,000 brands checked in the Ad Library. Warm up your email domains. Talk to 10 media-buying agencies. Answer 20 "AI video editor" job posts with your plan. Post 3 times a week.

  3. Days 31–60

    Sell. Email the list. Send about 10 spec ads a week. Close 4–8 Test Batches. Run 1–2 white-label pilots with agencies. Open Upwork and Contra profiles.

  4. Days 61–90

    Convert. Turn Test Batches into monthly plans. Publish 3 case studies with numbers. Add a referral offer. Check your real hours per ad and your margins, then adjust prices.

Realistic target by day 90 (est.)

2–4 monthly clients, about $5,000–$12,000 a month. 5–8 clients is a stretch goal.

What could go wrong?

Risks and how to handle them

RiskWhat to do
Prices keep falling (offshore editors, "AI discounts")Sell ideas, scripts and results, never hours. Pick 2–3 product categories and become known there.
Meta's own AI makes simple ads freeCompete on ideas, taste, cinematic quality and the monthly learning loop, not on variations.
A client gets in trouble for a fake testimonialFollow the rules above, get claims signed off, use the contract clauses.
An AI tool shuts down or changes price (Sora closed in 2026)Work with 2–3 models. Price per ad, not per second. Track your AI cost per finished second of video.
AI quality problems (logos, hands, lip sync)Start from real product photos, paste the real packshot in post, keep lines short, cut to b-roll often.
You run out of timeTemplates, locked characters, freelancers for variants and edits. Limit how many clients you take.
Clients leave after 2–3 months3-month minimum. Deliver a winning ad in the first 30 days. Monthly review. Offer a pause instead of a cancel. Healthy subscription churn is 3–7% a month.
The cheaper studio hurts Velrun's imageSeparate name and portfolio. "By the team behind Velrun" only in the footer and About page.
