The short answer

Cut a 60-second ad: hook, reel, problem, fix, proof and your face

A showreel proves taste. A buyer also needs to see what they get, why you, and that a real person stands behind the work. The cut below covers all of that in a minute, and its first three seconds still work as a silent cover image.

  • Open on one ad-grade shot, with text from the first frame

    The frame you choose becomes your cover in search, and sellers report that gig videos autoplay there. Browsers only autoplay without sound, so the hook has to work with the sound off.

  • Say it's AI in the hook

    "None of these ads were filmed" makes AI the idea. A University of Mississippi study (Aug 2026) found audiences accept AI ads more when AI is part of the idea than when it looks like a way to save money. The line is also your disclosure.

  • Show products the way Fiverr buyers sell them

    Home, pet, food and drink, accessories and beauty mood shots, as UGC demos, podcast ads, unboxings and product films. AI does best on product-focused ads (Ipsos and Syracuse, 2026). Keep the Velrun films for the proof beat.

  • Name the three ways cheap AI ads fail

    Buyers already fear ads that look fake, get the product wrong, or repeat one idea. Show all three in eight seconds, then show how you avoid them. That is the line between you and a $10 gig.

  • Sell a testing system

    One product, nine hooks, scripts included. Meta groups look-alike ads together, and about 6% of ads take most of the spend (Motion, 2026). Brands need different ideas to test, and a single polished video doesn't give them that.

  • Use your real numbers

    3.7 million views across two Fresh Forest spots is proof no $10 gig has. Fiverr requires the client's permission to show their work, so get it in writing, and do the same for Boston Dynamics before you name them.

  • End on your face

    Five to eight seconds of you, on camera, in your own voice. Fiverr recommends your own voice, a large clear human face was a key trust cue in a 500-million-impression study, and it answers the 62% who distrust AI people.

  • Ask for one easy thing

    "Send me your product link and I'll reply with three ad ideas." Sending a link is easier than choosing a package, and a fast, useful reply turns the message into an order.

  • Fix the rest of the gig too

    The video converts visits. The title, cover frame, starting price, packages and reply speed decide how many visits you get. See Gig page and Launch.

✓Where AI ads win

  • Product-focused ads. In the Ipsos and Syracuse test, AI came closest to human work on straightforward product ads.
  • Clicks. AI ads had a 0.76% click rate against 0.65% for human ads in Taboola's study, and fully AI ads got 19% more clicks in an NYU and Emory field test.
  • Speed and volume. Many different openings in days, which is what testing needs.

✕Where they lose

  • Emotion and storytelling. Fully AI remakes of 20 brand ads scored about 14% lower on short-term sales impact and 17% lower on long-term brand equity (Ipsos and Syracuse, 2026).
  • Fake people. 62% of US adults call AI people or voices in ads unacceptable even when disclosed (Gallup, May 2026).
  • What to tell buyers: AI for product demos, hooks and fast testing. Human creators or real footage for emotional testimonials.

What the competing gigs have in common

  • The same promises everywhere: realistic, hyper-realistic, 8K, TikTok, 24–48 hours, 20+ languages, and tool names such as Veo 3, Sora 2 and Arcads. Only one title in the sample mentions hooks.
  • Many high-review gigs are older gigs with new titles, so their reviews came from other services.
  • Niche specialists (skincare, fitness, supplements) only exist at about $10.
  • Cheap gigs lose money on good tools. A $10 order pays the seller $8 after Fiverr's 20% fee, less than one Arcads video costs (about $11).
  • At the top, Fiverr launched the AI Video Hub on 23 Mar 2026, a hand-picked list of AI directors such as The Dor Brothers and Billy Boman who sell cinema-quality commercials. That is the tier Velrun belongs in later.

Gaps you can own

  1. Hook test packs. One ad with several different openings, in three sizes.
  2. Product accuracy. Your real packshot in every shot, and a label check before delivery.
  3. Film direction. Every ad scripted and storyboarded first, by a studio with commissioned work.
  4. Rules done right. Licensed presenters, AI labels, and no fake testimonials.
  5. Checked European languages. German, French, Spanish, Italian and Polish versions, each checked by a native speaker.
  6. Monthly plans. Almost nobody sells them; Fiverr Subscriptions make it possible.

Rules for the motion graphics

  • Build the interface and the cards in a motion tool such as After Effects, Jitter or Rive. AI video models still garble text, and one broken word undercuts a video that sells quality. Use AI only for the clips inside the frames.
  • Draw your own simple interface in your studio colours. Don't screen-record Fiverr or show its logo or badges, and write "Meta" and "TikTok" as words instead of using their logos.
  • One typeface, two neutrals and one accent, and the same easing on every move. Each card stays up at least two seconds and holds seven words or fewer.
  • The steps must match your packages: the day-5 delivery and five hooks are the Standard package. Change the words if your packages change.
  • Make a 90-second version with every FAQ answer and attach it to your first reply to each message. Fiverr allows one video per gig, so this is where the long version goes.

✓Show

  • Invented brands, or real brands with written permission.
  • Products built from real product photos, with the real packshot laid over the scene.
  • Spoken lines under 8 seconds, with a cut to b-roll every 3–5 seconds.
  • An "AI presenter" tag on every realistic person.
  • Each 9:16 ad whole, inside a vertical panel.
  • "Made with AI" on the end card.

✕Leave out

  • Other brands' logos or products, and client work without written permission. That includes the Skittles marks on the Wingwalker.
  • Fake reviews, star ratings, "10,000 happy customers", AI before-and-after shots, AI doctors or experts.
  • Celebrity lookalikes, soundalikes, and cloned voices without consent.
  • Prices, links, emails, handles, QR codes and Fiverr badges.
  • "Hollywood quality", "100% realistic", "undetectable", and any sales or ROAS figure you can't document.
  • Tool names on screen. Tools change every quarter.
  • Stock footage. Fiverr counts it as not your own work.

One line at a time, seven words at most, 64–72 px tall at 1080p, in the middle third. Keep the bottom strip clear, where player controls appear.
Captions
Burn in every spoken line. Most buyers meet the video without sound first.
Voice
Record close to the microphone in a quiet, soft room. Sellers report that Fiverr compresses gig audio, so keep the voice dry and forward.
Mix
About −14 LUFS integrated and −1 dBTP true peak, with the music about 18 dB under the voice.
Music
Licensed for advertising, from Artlist or Epidemic Sound for example. Keep the license, since Fiverr requires permission for every audio file.

FAQ

Will it look like AI?

The presenters are AI and labelled as AI, but they look and sound natural. Every ad is scripted and storyboarded first, and I cut away before lines get long, which is where AI usually gives itself away.

Will my product look exactly right?

Yes. I start from your real product photos and lay your real packshot over the scene, so labels and logos stay sharp. Send 3–5 clear photos.

Can the presenter say they used my product?

No. AI presenters present your product; they don't pretend to be customers. US, UK and EU law forbids fake testimonials. I can show real customer reviews with your permission.

Can I run the ads on Meta, TikTok and YouTube?

Yes, you get commercial use for paid and organic ads. Switch on each platform's AI label when you upload. I also deliver versions with an on-screen AI note for New York and EU audiences.

Who owns the ads?

You get the finished ads for commercial use. Stock AI presenters aren't exclusive to you, and raw AI footage may not be protected by copyright, so the license covers the edited ads.

Which tools do you use?

Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.5 and HeyGen, with licensed avatars and voices. The mix changes as models improve.

What counts as a revision?

Changes to text, timing, music, voice or a shot. A new idea, a new presenter or a new script is a new concept.

Do you offer monthly plans?

Yes, as a Fiverr subscription or a custom offer: new ads every month, 10% off from the second month.

Launch

The first 30 days, and what to change when the numbers are low

  1. Days 1–4

    Make the shots, record the voice-over, cut Cut D (and the master for later), and make the three gallery images and two PDFs. Ask Fresh Forest in writing for permission to show their spot, and Boston Dynamics too if you want to name them.

  2. Day 5

    Publish the gig with the video. The video is reviewed for a few business days while the gig stays live. Once it's approved, set the cover frame.

  3. Days 5–30

    Be online during US and EU business hours. Answer every message within the hour with three ad ideas for the buyer's product, and send a custom offer the same day.

  4. Every week

    Check impressions, clicks and orders in Fiverr's analytics and use the table below. Change one thing at a time and give it two to three weeks.

  5. After 5 reviews

    Raise prices to the second number in each package. Turn on Subscriptions if your account qualifies; Fiverr says they add exposure.

  6. After Level 1

    Record the profile intro video.

  7. Month 3 and on

    Apply to Fiverr Pro with your commissioned work. Fiverr's AI Video Hub is a hand-picked roster, and Fiverr hasn't published how it chooses directors, so Pro vetting is the known way in. Then open the second gig with Cut C.
