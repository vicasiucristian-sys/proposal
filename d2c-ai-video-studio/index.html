Why brands need so many ads now

In December 2024 Meta launched Andromeda, a new system that chooses which ad each person sees. Three warnings

  • Meta is automating simple ads. Over 9 million small businesses use its AI creative tools (Q2 2026), and it can turn up to 20 product photos into an ad. Simple product-photo videos will become free. Your value is ideas, scripts and learning.
  • People trust AI presenters less than real people. Lab studies find human presenters win on trust and purchase intent, and 39% of Gen Z feel negative about AI ads. One agency owner reported AI ads that got clicks and leads but no sales, while real customer testimonial videos brought $20,000. Offer brands a mix: AI for hooks, scenes and product shots, and their real customers' videos for testimonials.
  • Automation is easy to copy. Public n8n templates and GitHub projects already generate UGC-style clips from a product page. Your edge is what they leave out: product accuracy, concept variety, claims control, quality checks and the testing loop.

Your quality checklist (publish it)

  1. Product: shape, label text, logo and colors match the packshot; real packshot on close-ups and the end card.
  2. People: hands, eyes, teeth and lip sync look right; cut any uncanny shot.
  3. Continuity: same face, clothes and product in every shot.
  4. Captions: built from the approved script and checked against a brand word list.
  5. Specs: hook in 3 seconds, 9:16 safe zones, length, loudness, file names.
  6. Claims: only claims from the client's approved list; no avatar as a "verified buyer".
  7. Rights: licensed presenters, voices and music; written consent for any real person.
  8. Labels: AI label where needed, platform AI settings on, AI metadata kept.
  9. Variety: each concept differs in angle, persona, setting or format.
  10. Records: prompts, tools and approvals saved for each ad.

How do you make scripts worth paying for?

Scripts are your product. The pipeline just makes them visible

AI writes a script in seconds, and Fiverr sellers charge $35–$40. Professionals still get $150–$800 per script, and one supplement brand pays $400 per test script plus $500–$1,000 for each script that wins. They pay for research, angles and testing. With production automated, your scripts are the main thing clients buy from you, and the main use of your time.

What's in one script

  • Header: goal, customer type, awareness stage, angle, offer, product claims and their proof.
  • 3 hooks from 3 different angles, not 3 rewordings.
  • Body and call to action.
  • Voiceover, word for word, and on-screen text.
  • Shot list for your pipeline: shots of 3–8 seconds, product shots from real photos.
  • 15 s and 30 s versions.

What wins on Meta in 2026

  • Formats with the most winners (Motion, 550k+ ads): offer-first, demo, testimonial, unboxing, montage, listicle, split-screen, us-vs-them, before and after (often rejected in health and beauty).
  • Hooks used by accounts spending $1M+ a month: confession, bold claim, relatability, contrast, curiosity.
  • Good numbers: hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) 30% or more; hold rate about 25%. About 5% of ads become winners.
  • Scripted "testimonial" UGC now feels predictable. Street interviews, skits, expert comments and founder stories are working.

Your monthly process

  1. Research. Customer reviews, Reddit, Amazon and TikTok comments, competitors' ads in the Meta Ad Library. Agents collect; you choose.
  2. Angle map. Customer types × awareness stage × desire.
  3. Scripts. Agents draft; you pick the angle and sharpen the hook.
  4. Production. Nothing is generated before the script is approved.
  5. Testing. The brand or its media buyer runs new ads in a test campaign and judges them after 3–7 days.
  6. Next batch. About 60% new versions of winners, 30% winners in new formats, 10% brand-new ideas.
  7. Monthly memo. What won, what we learned, what we test next.

Why brands pay you instead of using ChatGPT

  • Better input: real customer words, ad results, support tickets, reasons for returns.
  • Truly different ideas, which Meta's system rewards.
  • A claims check on every script.
  • A learning loop: every month's results shape the next scripts.
  • Accountability: you report the share of winning ads against the ~5% average. Optional: a bonus for each winning ad.

What's legal?

AI ads are legal. Fake customers are not

Most risk comes from AI people who look like real customers, and from health or beauty claims. Checked again on 25 Sep 2026. This is research, not legal advice. Ask a lawyer to check your contract before your first clients sign.

Dates that matter

  1. 21 Oct 2024

    US: the FTC rule on reviews and testimonials takes effect. It bans testimonials from people who don't exist, including AI-made ones. Fines are over $50,000 per violation.

  2. 6 Apr 2025

    UK: the DMCC Act bans fake reviews. The UK ad regulator also says an AI label can't fix a misleading before/after image (May 2025).

  3. 1 Jun 2026

    Meta: automatically detects media made with outside AI tools and shows "AI info" on the ad. You don't have to declare AI on normal product ads, but don't remove AI metadata.

  4. 9 Jun 2026

    New York: ads that use an AI "synthetic performer" must say so clearly. Fines: $1,000, then $5,000 per violation. Law firms say both the brand and the studio are exposed.

  5. 9 Jul 2026

    Google Ads: new AI label for commercial ads, set in the ad or with an "AI label" setting. Viewers see "How this ad was made".

  6. 2 Aug 2026

    EU: AI Act Article 50 applies. Realistic AI people count as deepfakes and must be labeled. Fines up to €15M or 3% of turnover. Not delayed.

  7. Now

    TikTok: ads with AI-generated or heavily AI-edited media must use the "AI-generated content" disclaimer or their own label. No real person's likeness in AI endorsements.

✓Do

  • Write avatar ads as brand presenter ads, not customer testimonials.
  • If an AI presenter repeats a real review, keep the review, its date and the customer's permission on file, and say "AI presenter" on screen and in the audio.
  • Label realistic AI people for EU and New York audiences; turn on TikTok's AI disclaimer and Google's AI label.
  • Keep the tools' watermarks and AI metadata in the files.
  • Use licensed stock presenters and voices. Get written consent from any real person.
  • Get the client to sign off the proof for every product claim before production.
  • Match tool plans to your revenue (for example Midjourney Pro above $1M revenue); never use free plans for client work.

✕Don't

  • Invent customer stories, star ratings or numbers like "10,000 happy customers".
  • Make AI doctors, nurses or experts.
  • Make AI before/after results, or AI-improved skin and bodies.
  • Use celebrity or creator lookalikes, soundalike voices, or cloned voices without consent.
  • Promise copyright in raw AI footage, or promise better sales or CPA than human UGC.
  • Prompt for other brands' logos or characters, or a living artist's style.
  • Write "Are you struggling with…?" lines. Meta rejects ads that imply a viewer's health or personal traits.

In the EU you are usually the one who must label

Law firms reading the European Commission's 2026 guidelines say the duty to label falls on whoever decides how AI is used. When your pipeline makes the AI people, that's you. Deliver labeled master files and write in the contract who does what.

Put this in your client contract

  1. Scope: concepts, ads, lengths, formats and languages in each plan.
  2. Revisions, and what counts as a new concept.
  3. Delivery times, counted from an approved script.
  4. Payment in advance, minimum term, notice period, pause rules.
  5. Rights: the client gets the deliverables on full payment "to the extent rights exist". Raw AI footage may not be protected by copyright; your scripts and edits are.
  6. Licensed presenters, voices and music: what each license covers and for how long.
  7. Client promises: rights to their materials, proof for their claims, real reviews with permission.
  8. AI labels: who labels what; the client keeps labels on and turns on platform AI settings.
  9. No performance guarantee: no promises on ROAS, click rates or ad approval.
  10. Liability limit (fees of the last 3–12 months) and indemnities both ways.

How do you get clients?

Sales is now your real bottleneck, so build it first

Your pipeline can serve about 5 clients alone and 10 with one helper. To get there you need a steady flow of new clients, and new ones to replace those who leave (3–7% a month is healthy for subscriptions). These steps come from founders who built productized creative businesses.

Your first-10-clients playbook

  1. Fix your offer first: public plans, what counts as an ad, delivery times.
  2. Make 5 spec ads for real brands in one category. Publish each with a short breakdown: the angle, the hooks, the result.
  3. Send each spec ad privately to that brand's founder or growth lead, with the $990 Test Batch.
  4. Offer free hook teardowns: review a brand's current ads and remake their weakest opening within 48 hours.
  5. Post short and often on LinkedIn and X: spec ads, teardowns, hook tests.
  6. Answer "AI video editor" job posts with your plan as the alternative.
  7. Partner with 3–5 media-buying agencies as their white-label creative team.
  8. Get listed on TikTok One's Partner Exchange, Upwork and Contra for early reviews.
  9. Start with founding-client prices, then raise them as you fill up. Designjoy started at $449 and now charges $4,995.
  10. Stay selective: fewer, better clients churn less.

Who do you hire first?

A salesperson or account manager before another editor

Contract must-haves

  • NDA for briefs, products and results.
  • All work, files and prompts assigned to your company.

  • No poaching clients; turnaround and revision rules; pay per approved deliverable.

How do you set up the company in Romania?

Start as an SRL; plan for profit tax once you pass €100,000

At the "alone, 5 clients" example (about €196,000 a year) you'd be above the micro limit. An SRL keeps your personal assets separate from claims about AI content, which matters in this business. Ask your accountant whether a PFA's lower tax is worth the personal risk.

VAT

  • Selling to EU businesses: no Romanian VAT; reverse charge. Check their VAT number in VIES, get an Art. 317 VAT code before the first invoice if you aren't VAT-registered, and file D390 monthly.
  • Selling to UK and US businesses: not taxable in Romania; no VAT on the invoice.
  • Registration threshold: 395,000 lei (about €77,000) since Sep 2025. Sources disagree on whether services sold abroad count; ask your accountant.
  • Buying US and EU AI tools: you pay 21% Romanian VAT yourself (reverse charge, form D301), even without VAT registration. If you register for VAT voluntarily, that VAT cancels out. With your tool spend, this is worth discussing.
  • Add your Romanian VAT code to every vendor's billing profile.

Getting paid and protected

  • Stripe works for Romanian companies: about 1.5% + €0.25 for EU cards, 2.5% for UK cards, 3.25% for other international cards, plus about 2% for currency conversion; Stripe Billing +0.7%. Check live prices.
  • Wise Business for bank transfers: free local USD, GBP and EUR accounts, conversion from about 0.33%.
  • US clients will ask for form W-8BEN-E. Services done fully outside the US normally have no US withholding.
  • Contracts: a master agreement plus an order form per plan. Free templates: Common Paper, Bonterms. Add AI, likeness and labeling clauses.
  • Insurance: professional liability from about €250–300 a year in Romania (Groupama, Omniasig). Ask in writing whether AI-generated content claims are covered.
  • Trademark: EUIPO €850 for one class; the SME Fund may refund part.

Ask your accountant

  • Micro or profit-tax SRL from the start, if you expect to pass €100,000 in year 1?
  • Full VAT registration or just the Art. 317 code, given your AI tool spend?
  • Do services sold abroad count toward the 395,000 lei threshold?
  • Withholding tax on payments abroad for music, voices or stock footage?
  • Invoicing in USD, EUR and GBP, and any tax changes still pending for 2026–27.

What do you do first?

The first 90 days

  1. Days 1–14

    Set up. Check the name at ONRC and register the SRL. Decide on VAT with an accountant. Open Stripe and Wise. Contract templates. Publish your quality checklist and AI policy. Make 12 spec ads across 3–4 product categories with your pipeline.

  2. Days 15–30

    Launch. Website live with prices. List of 1,000–2,000 brands checked in the Ad Library. Warm up email domains. Join 2–3 communities. Apply to TikTok's Partner Exchange. Talk to 10 media-buying agencies. Answer 20 "AI video editor" job posts. Post 3 times a week.

  3. Days 31–60

    Sell. Email the list. Do about 10 free hook teardowns a week. Close 4–8 Test Batches. Run 1–2 white-label pilots. Open Upwork and Contra profiles.

  4. Days 61–90

    Convert. Turn Test Batches into monthly plans. Publish 3 case studies with numbers. Hold day-30 reviews with every client. Check your real hours and AI cost per ad, then adjust prices.

Realistic target by day 90 (est.)

2–5 monthly clients, about $5,000–$15,000 a month. Your pipeline could serve more; how many brands you reach decides the rest.
